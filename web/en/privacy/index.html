Bazibo privacy policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026 (Letters and the read-aloud voice; before that: Move progress and the camera; before that: Telling Time and the roadmap page; before that: Colouring; the website at bazibo.nl; before that: Snake, save data and backup, the name Bazibo, Dogudoku, Picturedoku and Four in a Row)

Bazibo is an app of games for children (Bubble Shooter, Dogudoku, Picturedoku, Four in a Row, Snake, Colouring, Telling Time, Letters and more), made by Mark Westerweel (westerweel.work). In short: Bazibo collects no data about you or your child.

What we don't do

  • No ads.
  • No account, no sign-in, no name or email address needed.
  • No analytics, no trackers, no third-party software watching along.
  • The app itself sends nothing over the internet: no data to us and none to anyone else.

Save data: what stays on the device

The app remembers, on the device itself, how many stars each level or puzzle got, how often Four in a Row was won, a few game settings (such as automatic crosses in Dogudoku, numbers instead of pictures in Picturedoku, or the chosen board and tokens in Four in a Row, or the last chosen level in Telling Time, or capitals in Letters), which how-to cards were already shown, the colours a child put in a colouring page, whether sound is on or off, the chosen language and whether the full version was bought. That never reaches us.

In the Android and iPhone app this save data is kept in the app's own settings storage, so it is part of the phone's own backup: Android backup to your Google account (and the transfer to a new Android phone), or the iPhone's iCloud or computer backup, depending on how you set up your phone. That way the progress comes along to a new phone, without an account. Google or Apple handle that backup under your own account; we cannot see it. Delete the app (or clear the site data in your browser) and the data on the device is gone.

The read-aloud voice

In Letters a voice says letters and words. That voice was made beforehand with Piper, a program that runs on our own computer, and ships as small sound files inside the app. Nothing is sent to a speech service or to the internet, and the app does not listen: no microphone is used.

English voice: Piper en_GB-cori, trained on the Hi-Fi Multi-Speaker English TTS dataset (NVIDIA; Cori Samuel, LibriVox), CC BY 4.0; audio synthesised from that model. Dutch voice: Piper nl_NL-alex, trained on an Open Home Foundation voice dataset (CC0).

Moving progress and the camera

Under Settings › Move progress (behind the question for parents) you can take the progress from one device to another. One device shows the stars, colouring pages and game settings as QR codes; the other scans them with its camera, or you paste the code as text. Nothing goes over the internet and nothing comes to us. The purchase does not travel: on the other device you get it back with "Restore purchase".

For this the app asks permission to use the camera. The camera is only on while the "Receive" screen is open, and is only used to read the QR code. No photos or images are kept or sent. If you do not give permission, moving progress still works by pasting the code.

Buying the full version

Every game has a free part (in the bubble shooter: levels 1 to 10). The full version, which opens everything in every game, can be bought once in the app. Payment goes entirely through Google Play or the Apple App Store. We see no name, no payment details and no account: the app only asks the store, on the device, whether the purchase exists. For that part, Google's or Apple's privacy policy applies. Buying and restoring sit behind a question for parents, so a child cannot buy anything by accident.

The website

The web version of Bazibo is hosted at bazibo.nl by the hosting company Yourhosting, with a copy on GitHub Pages. Like any web server, both keep technical data such as your IP address, the time and the page requested in log files. Those exist for security and troubleshooting; we use them for nothing else. For GitHub, GitHub's privacy statement applies. The app itself uses neither.

Children

Bazibo is made for children. Because the app collects nothing, no children's data is processed either.
